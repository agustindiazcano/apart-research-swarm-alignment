# Hackathon Rules and Logistics (working summary)

Status: compiled on 2026-11-08 from the organizer email and two organizer logistics talks. The Guidelines tab of the sprint page is the authority. Verify every item against it before submitting. Items marked (talk) come from spoken statements and may be approximate.

## 1. Deadline

- Sunday, November 8, 2026, 11:59 PM AoE (Anywhere on Earth, UTC-12).
- Equals Monday, November 9, 2026, 11:59 UTC.
- Equals Monday, November 9, 2026, 08:59 in Argentina (UTC-3).
- After the deadline the submit button disappears. No new submissions and no edits.
- (talk) The organizer may allow a grace period of about 1 to 2 hours to fix broken PDFs, but explicitly said not to rely on it.

## 2. Deliverable

- One research report in PDF, on the official template from the Guidelines tab (a Google Drive document to copy and export).
- Length: 4 to 8 pages, excluding references and appendices. 8 pages is a hard cap.
- File size: up to 25 MB.
- Required appendix: "Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations". State what the setup or findings could make easier to carry out or harder to detect.
- Optional uploads: presentation recording, slides, code, images, project cover image.

## 3. Judging rubric (three dimensions)

1. Impact potential and innovation: how impactful the idea is, and whether it is new to the field or only replicates recent work.
2. Execution quality: soundness of methodology, implementation and findings.
3. Presentation and clarity: how clearly the work, findings and impact are communicated.

Not scored (talk): technical expertise, code quality, whether swarm alignment is "solved".
All tracks use the same rubric. Tracks orient the project but do not change scoring.
Judges are matched by report content, not by selected track. More than one judge may review a report.

## 4. Rules that can disqualify

- Undisclosed prior work. Building on existing work is allowed. Mark pre-existing code as such in both the README and the report, and state what is new from the sprint. Only sprint work is judged. The organizer checks for undisclosed prior work.
- Fabricated results or references (including AI-hallucinated citations and metrics).
- Projects not relevant to the event theme. Projects may span tracks or sit outside the four tracks if relevant.
- Plagiarism.
- More than two projects per person (maximum is two).

## 5. Clarity and AI usage

- AI tools are allowed without restriction. The team is responsible for everything in the report.
- A judge may decline to score a report that is too unclear to follow. This is not a disqualification, but it yields no score.
- (talk) Organizers say judges, who are volunteers, have low tolerance for padded or machine-sounding text and that such text is easy to spot. Recommended practice: draft with AI if useful, then reread and rewrite by hand. A report with little content spread over 8 pages is a risk.

## 6. Eligibility and registration

- Register on the sprint page. Only registered teams can submit and win prizes.
- The pre-hackathon survey (about 2 minutes) is required for a team to be eligible for prizes. One response per person.
- Teams of 1 to 5. (talk) Winning teams tend to have 1 to 3 members.
- Team registration is optional before submission. Team details are entered in the submission form.

## 7. Submission procedure

Form fields: project title, short summary (what was done, what was found), PDF, interest in publishing, track(s), team name and country, member names, emails, Discord usernames, Google Scholar profile (optional). Optional: recording, slides, code, images.

- Refresh the page right before submitting (the site may be updated during the weekend).
- A confirmation email with a private link arrives after submitting. The link can take about 10 minutes to start working.
- Submit an early version, then resubmit with the exact same title to replace it. Only the final version at the deadline is judged.
- Projects are not public on the website until judging ends, because of information hazards.
- (talk) If a code repository may contain hazardous information, keep it private unless the organizer asks otherwise. It was unclear how judges access private repositories. Ask in the help-desk if this applies.

## 8. Judging and prizes

- 50 or more volunteer judges (talk). Judging starts right after the grace period and does not pause.
- Expected volume: about 250 submissions (talk, organizer estimate).
- Prizes: first USD 2,000, second USD 1,000, third USD 600, fourth USD 200, fifth USD 200. Total USD 4,000.
- Referral: a referred person who wins earns the referrer the same amount (do not form the same team).
- Winners announced about 1 to 2 weeks after judging. All participants receive reviewer feedback by email.
- Top teams may be invited to the Apart studio (a few weeks funded for a research proposal), then the fellowship, and may be shortlisted for partner fellowships.

## 9. Compute and tools

- Adaption Labs provides USD 500 worth of credits plus 3 months of premium access per participant. Winning-team members receive USD 1,000 in credits. Sign up at the Adaption app with the same email used for the sprint.
- The displayed balance varies between participants (talk: reports of 1,000 to 2,000 credits, possibly from a second hackathon). Do not cite a specific credit amount in the report without checking the account.
- Using Adaption is not required.
- There are no API credits from model providers. OpenRouter free tiers exist but are rate-limited (talk).
- There are no provided datasets or sandbox harnesses. Building the dataset or environment is part of the challenge. Check the Resources tab for example projects and tools.
